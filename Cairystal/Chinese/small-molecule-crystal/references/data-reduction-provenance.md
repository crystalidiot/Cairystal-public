# Data reduction: preserve the evidence chain

Use this when an alert or poor refinement suggests an acquisition, indexing, scaling, absorption or merging problem. Reading a historical guide is not permission to operate a diffractometer. Default to offline copies; do not move a goniometer, operate shutters, reset devices, clear calibration files or change generator/cryo settings.

## Ask what data actually exist

HKL plus INS/RES can support solution/refinement but cannot generally reconstruct integration. Inventory raw frames, experiment and instrument parameters/corrections, run lists, integration profiles, reduction logs, hardware/scale plots, crystal images and the matching experimental CIF. Missing raw evidence means report the limitation, not fabricate a corrected measurement. Archive the original experiment; do not copy calibration from an unrelated machine.

## Separate stages and versions

For third-party frame import, opening an image is only the first check. Verify wavelength, detector geometry/pixel size, beam centre, scan axes and direction, run/frame naming and the provenance of any parameter-file override. Overlay the beamstop/shadow mask on actual frames: a default top-entry mask may not fit another instrument. Do not invent geometry, enlarge a mask to improve refinement statistics, or silently replace measured header values with tutorial defaults. Save the original headers and the reason for every override. If these checks require missing calibration or raw images, request the instrument operator's matching records rather than promising an integration repair from HKL/INS alone.

- Indexing and integration establish reciprocal-space assignments and integrated observations. Refinalization reuses integrated observations for symmetry/scaling/absorption/uncertainty/export choices; it does not necessarily reintegrate the raw frames.
- Quick screening or automatic early solution is provisional, not final data acceptance. Acquisition may continually overwrite outputs; save a dated snapshot and identify whether collection finished.
- Unit-cell similarity in a database provides candidates, not chemical identity or space-group proof. Unindexed peaks can reflect another domain, contamination, modulation or an inadequate model; display filters do not prove absence.
- Track every change with input hashes, settings, logs and independent output names. Keep HKL, INS, CIF, cell/setting, Friedel handling and geometry consistent. An old experimental CIF must not overwrite a transformed final model indiscriminately.

## Require evidence before rejecting observations

Inspect raw images and run/frame plots for shadowing, ice, overload, miscentering, damage or scale jumps. Record affected ranges and the reason. Compare completeness (overall and outer shell), redundancy, I/sigma, merging statistics and Friedel coverage before/after filtering. A lower Rint or fewer alerts alone does not justify rejection. Display colour, contrast, hidden peak groups and resolution rings are diagnostic aids, not changes to measured intensities.

Keep rejection layers separate: integration flags, export filtering, SHEL resolution exclusions and explicit refinement OMIT are not interchangeable counts. Exporter reason flags can overlap on one observation; report their union and intersections rather than summing them. A strong flagged observation is not automatically valid, and a zero/nonpositive sigma in a rejected-record table must not be treated as a usable precision estimate. Match setting, symmetry and repeated observations before connecting an upstream rejected hkl to a later OMIT. Prefer a coherent LST accounting over an appended GUI summary that labels an input-record count as refined unique data; this mismatch occurs in the supplied disorder7 example (13214 input versus1757 refined unique).

Also separate observations selected to fit a correction model from observations rejected from its output. In the reviewed SADABS Bi/Br example, 7926 of9839 observations determine correction parameters, but8970 are written: the final rejection is869, not1913. The independent groups change3451→3442; nine lost groups do not mean nine rejected observations. Scaling equivalents defined with inversion do not by themselves establish that the exported HKL was Friedel-merged; this log explicitly writes unmerged records. Scope each Rint/wR2(int) to the stated selection and processing stage before comparing it with refinement residuals.

Expected formula can affect absorption calculations. If the composition changes after solution, examine whether reduction needs revision; changing only reported metadata may be insufficient. Numerical shape-based absorption needs an actual usable shape model, not invented faces or dimensions. More fitted correction parameters need validation against overfitting.

For empirical scaling, identify the proposed physical source (absorption, illumination/miscentering, detector response or time-dependent decay) before adding flexibility. Fewer frames per scale, looser scale restraints or higher spherical-harmonic orders can reduce Rint by fitting noise. Compare physically plausible scale curves and downstream refinement on consistently defined observations; neither minimum Rint nor minimum model R1 alone is an acceptance criterion. Preserve the unmodified integration output and correction settings. Shape-based correction requires calibrated multi-view images, a usable closed geometry and matching beam/centering information; a powder-capillary transmission estimate is not a measured single-crystal Tmin/Tmax.

## Twin and symmetry boundary

A reported versus embedded reflection-count difference is a diagnostic question, not automatically a broken CIF. Reconcile the exact model's LST input, SHEL exclusions, systematic absences, retained unmerged observations and merged unique set. In the supplied Co/Br pair, 5716−574−22=5120 and 5286−12=5274 explain those differences without editing reflections or metadata. Conversely, do not invent exclusions to force agreement when the matching log is missing. For an apparent transmission mismatch, inspect the actual correction log and spherical μr/shape assumptions before changing Tmin/Tmax; agreement with a dimension-based estimate is not a reason to overwrite documented processing values.

Distinguish peak-profile training selection, cumulative integration rejection counters, correction/error-model fitting selection and export filtering. Do not sum successive cumulative counters. A saved RRF/filter definition does not prove it was applied; compare the active log and output counts. If stage arithmetic leaves a discrepancy, retain it explicitly instead of inventing a missing origin/terminator. An OFF switch for one rejection layer does not disable all upstream rejection. In the supplied modulation example, the finalising UB cell differs from the final statistics/P4P cell, while the experimental CIF also stores unconstrained cell fields: preserve each role and reconcile the lineage before using cell values in a report. Zero uncertainty placeholders do not establish exact measurements.

For a novice-facing rescue proposal, show the tradeoff, not only the improved residual. In the historical V0021 ice example, interval/run filtering gave R1 4.11% with 92.6% completeness, while a profile-based filter gave R1 4.50% with 98.6% completeness (automatic processing: 6.11% and 99.9%). These are reported tutorial comparisons, not reproduced experiments or universal filter settings. Require actual contamination/profile evidence and retain rejection lists; never copy the tutorial's d-range or I/sigma cutoffs blindly. If missing coverage needs new measurement, identify that dependency rather than claiming HKL/INS can restore unmeasured observations. Signal-to-uncertainty heuristics do not guarantee a final R1.

Inventory corrections already applied before stacking new absorption methods. A numerical shape correction and a further spherical approximation may double-correct the same effect; empirical residual scaling is a separate question that must be evaluated with the actual implementation. In V0021's W example, adding a spherical correction after empirical+face correction lowered Rint 3.5→3.3% but worsened R1 2.99→5.58% and shifted W3 Ueq substantially. Do not select that output solely for its lower merging residual. A single exp(-mu×size) estimate is an attenuation illustration, not a measured full-crystal transmission range; inspect path/shape/illumination and matching composition, wavelength and units.

Distinguish uncertainty calibration against equivalent observations from SHELXL weighting and extinction refinement. A changed error model changes I/sigma, not the recorded signal; do not inflate uncertainties merely to force a unit goodness of fit. Keep valid weak and background-subtracted negative observations unless a documented measurement problem warrants rejection. When comparing successive results, record changed reflection counts/cutoffs and parameter counts (including EXTI), not just R1. A sixth HKL column can encode a run/batch rather than a twin component; establish the exporter semantics before assigning HKLF5.

Distinguish simultaneous domains from a single crystal that changes orientation during collection. Multiple UB matrices fitted to the aggregate peak list do not resolve that distinction; inspect frame/run-dependent peak positions and orientation history. Check the absolute number and distribution of independently indexed peaks as well as percentages: a high overall index rate can conceal a near-overlap centroid error, and a small extra domain can merely fit noise. Tutorial index-rate thresholds are diagnostic hints, not pass/fail rules.

Keep three different meanings of mask separate: detector shadow/beamstop exclusion, the integration region around each reflection, and a real-space solvent contribution to calculated structure factors. Shrinking the integration region can reduce the reported overlap fraction by changing classification, without changing the crystal. Require peak-profile/tail, intensity and downstream model checks before adopting such a change; do not optimize only the overlap percentage or reuse an example's 0.5/0.75 factor by default.

Read the actual exporter settings and resulting file format. Domain separation, overlap thresholds, independent scales and a combined HKLF4 export can change the observation model; do not treat this as interchangeable with HKLF5 or assume component fractions are transferred correctly. Historical default thresholds are not universal settings. Preserve non-overlapped, partly overlapped and fully overlapped evidence where available.

Audit processing logs by run/stage, not just basename: an appended twin log can contain multiple UB settings, different rejection counts and a repeated earlier symmetry-analysis block. Distinguish numeric HKLF5 component records from measured groups; negative component identifiers precede the positive terminal record of one group, so sorting or deduplicating individual lines destroys the observation model. An export-footer BASF is an initial estimate, not necessarily the refined BASF. Compare the actual embedded HKL and active RES with the validation report, including counts, cell, weights and parameter number. In the supplied 5F example, 7237 component records encode 5767 groups, whereas the final-named CIF embeds 8549 records/6874 groups; a nearby CHK matches neither complete current model signature. A filename such as finalcif does not resolve that lineage. See [official SHELXL HKLF/BASF semantics](https://shelx.uni-goettingen.de/shelxl_html.php).

Systematic-absence statistics, metric symmetry, E-statistics and Rint provide complementary evidence, not a one-button space-group certificate. Do not remove allegedly forbidden reflections before validating the extinction hypothesis, then use their removal to confirm it. Record any cell transformation across reflections, coordinates, ADPs and twin laws.

Trace nonnegative/zero-rich intensities upstream before diagnosing deliberate clipping. A TWINABS-extracted, detwinned HKLF4 can differ substantially from the paired summed-intensity HKLF5; do not apply the original BASF to already detwinned data, compare their raw R values as equivalent trials, or manufacture negative values to clear PLAT961. The supplied hemicyanine17 HKLF4 already has no negatives before its later setting conversion, while HKLF5 retains negatives. Extra zeros and small scale/precision changes during export require their own accounting; their mechanism is not established merely by counting them. Preserve reduction, export and final-refinement transformations separately. See [IUCr TWINABS workflow](https://journals.iucr.org/d/issues/2019/12/00/rr5182/).
